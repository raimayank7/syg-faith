Namaz time for your city

Prayer times depend on where you are and what day it is, because they follow the sun. Dhuhr is at solar noon; Maghrib is at sunset; Fajr and Isha are decided by how far below the horizon the sun is, and that is where the calculation methods differ. Choose a city, check the method and the Asr rule, and the table gives each time in both 24-hour and 12-hour clocks, marks the next prayer and lets you copy or share the day, or print the whole month.

Every city has a page with the same tool, the Qibla bearing and distance to the Kaaba, and a table for four seasons. New to how the times are worked out? Read how prayer times are calculated. Everything is calculated on your device; nothing about you is stored or sent.

Questions people ask

Which method should I choose?

Choose the one your mosque or community uses. If you do not know, the default for your region is used: Karachi in India, ISNA in the United States and Canada, Umm al-Qura in the Gulf, Muslim World League elsewhere.

What is the difference between the Asr options?

Most schools begin Asr when the shadow of an object equals its length plus the noon shadow. The Hanafi school waits until it is twice the length plus the noon shadow, which is later by 30 to 60 minutes.

Are these times the same as my mosque's?

They can differ by a few minutes: mosques usually add a margin of caution and list the iqamah, the time the congregation begins, not the start of the prayer.
