Eid greetings

How to say Eid Mubarak and other Eid greetings in Arabic, Urdu, Hindi, Bengali, Turkish, Malay and other languages, and how to reply.

The most common greeting is "Eid Mubarak" (عيد مبارك), meaning "blessed Eid". It is understood nearly everywhere and the reply is the same. Others are:

  • Arabic: "Eid Mubarak", or "Kul am wa antum bi-khair" (may you be well every year).
  • Urdu and Hindi: "Eid Mubarak" or "Eid ki mubarakbad".
  • Bengali: "Eid Mubarak" (ঈদ মোবারক).
  • Turkish: "Bayramınız mübarek olsun" for the festival (bayram).
  • Malay and Indonesian: "Selamat Hari Raya Aidilfitri" for Eid al-Fitr; Indonesians also say "Mohon maaf lahir dan batin", "forgive me, outer and inner".

In writing a message, keep it short and warm. A line such as "Wishing you and your family a blessed Eid full of peace and joy" is enough. Eid al-Adha greetings are the same, and in India people also say "Bakrid Mubarak". These phrases are widely used; if you are not sure about a local custom, "Eid Mubarak" is always safe.

Dates are expected dates from the Umm al-Qura calendar. The real day of Eid is fixed by the sighting of the moon, and can be a day later (often in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and elsewhere). Please follow your local announcement. This page is not a religious ruling; ask your local mosque or a trusted scholar.
