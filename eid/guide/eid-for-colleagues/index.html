Eid for colleagues and neighbours

A short, practical guide for non-Muslim colleagues, teachers and neighbours on how to be thoughtful around Eid: timing, greetings, food and leave.

Eid is one of the happiest days in the year for Muslims, and a few simple habits make it better for the people you work and live with.

  • Know the date late: Eid is decided by the moon, so the day may be confirmed only the night before. Allow a day's flexibility for leave if possible.
  • Greet: "Eid Mubarak" is welcome.
  • Food: many people bring sweets to the office the next working day; accept them gladly. If you host a lunch, remember that Eid al-Adha features meat, and that halal and vegetarian options matter.
  • Do not schedule exams or key meetings on or right after Eid, if you can avoid it.
  • Neighbours: a sweet dish sent across with a short note is a lovely gesture, and an invitation is an honour.

Dates are expected dates from the Umm al-Qura calendar. The real day of Eid is fixed by the sighting of the moon, and can be a day later (often in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and elsewhere). Please follow your local announcement. This page is not a religious ruling; ask your local mosque or a trusted scholar.
