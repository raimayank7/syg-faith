Eid dates 2033

Expected dates of Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha in 2033.

The Islamic calendar is lunar and about eleven days shorter than the Gregorian year, so the dates move earlier each year; in some Gregorian years an Eid falls twice.

How 2033 compares with 2032

The Islamic year has 354 or 355 days, about eleven days fewer than the Gregorian year, so each festival comes about eleven days earlier in the calendar every year, until it has gone all the way round the seasons in about 33 years. For 2033: Eid al-Fitr is 11 days earlier in the year than in 2032 (then Wednesday 14 January 2032); Day of Arafat is 10 days earlier in the year than in 2032 (then Sunday 21 March 2032); Eid al-Adha is 10 days earlier in the year than in 2032 (then Monday 22 March 2032); Ramadan begins is 11 days earlier in the year than in 2032 (then Saturday 4 December 2032).

Ramadan starting November to Eid al-Fitr

The fast of Ramadan is expected to begin on Wednesday 23 November 2033 and Eid al-Fitr to fall on Friday 23 December 2033, 30 days later (a month of 29 or 30 days, depending on the moon). If you are planning leave, a school holiday or travel, remember that the real day may be a day later and, for Eid itself, is often announced only the evening before.

The days around Eid al-Adha

The Day of Arafat is expected on Friday 11 March 2033, the day before Eid al-Adha on Saturday 12 March 2033. Pilgrims on hajj stand at Arafat that day, and many Muslims who are not on pilgrimage fast on it. Eid al-Adha begins a festival of three or four days in which families who can afford it sacrifice an animal and share the meat with relatives and the poor. Because the announcement depends on the sighting of the moon in Dhul Hijjah, the Gregorian dates can move by a day.

The gap between the two Eids this year is 68 days, about ten weeks: Eid al-Fitr falls on 1 Shawwal and Eid al-Adha on 10 Dhul Hijjah, two months and nine days later.

How to use these dates

Use the date for planning only, and confirm the final day with your own mosque, committee or the national announcement. Offices and schools in India usually publish the holiday list for the year in advance, using these expected dates, and later move the holiday by a day if the moon is seen late. Greeting a Muslim friend or colleague with “Eid Mubarak” is warmly received on either day.

Dates are expected dates from the Umm al-Qura calendar. The real day of Eid is fixed by the sighting of the moon, and can be a day later (often in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and elsewhere). Please follow your local announcement. This page is not a religious ruling; ask your local mosque or a trusted scholar.
