The ten Sikh Gurus

From Guru Nanak Dev Ji to Guru Gobind Singh Ji, and the Guru Granth Sahib.

The Sikh tradition begins with Guru Nanak Dev Ji, born in 1469 in Talwandi (now Nankana Sahib, in Pakistan). Ten Gurus in all gave the teaching shape over about two hundred and thirty years. The tenth Guru, Guru Gobind Singh Ji, declared before his passing in 1708 that after him the Guru would be the Guru Granth Sahib, the scripture, and the Khalsa Panth. The years below are those commonly given.

GuruYearsKnown for
Guru Nanak Dev Ji1469 to 1539Founder of Sikhism; Japji Sahib; the message of one God, honest work, sharing and remembering the Divine Name
Guru Angad Dev Ji1504 to 1552Popularised the Gurmukhi script and carried on the community kitchen (langar) begun by Guru Nanak Dev Ji
Guru Amar Das Ji1479 to 1574Anand Sahib; strengthened langar and the system of preaching centres (manjis)
Guru Ram Das Ji1534 to 1581Founded the city of Amritsar; composed hymns including the wedding hymns (Laavan)
Guru Arjan Dev Ji1563 to 1606Compiled the Adi Granth in 1604; Sukhmani Sahib; built Harmandir Sahib (the Golden Temple)
Guru Hargobind Sahib Ji1595 to 1644Introduced the idea of spiritual and worldly authority together (miri-piri) and trained the Sikhs in arms
Guru Har Rai Ji1630 to 1661Known for compassion and for medical care for the sick
Guru Har Krishan Ji1656 to 1664The youngest Guru, who became Guru as a child and cared for the sick in Delhi
Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji1621 to 1675His hymns are in the Guru Granth Sahib; he gave his life defending the freedom of religion
Guru Gobind Singh Ji1666 to 1708Founded the Khalsa in 1699 and declared the Guru Granth Sahib to be the Guru after him

Verses by the Gurus in daily banis

Japji Sahib is by Guru Nanak Dev Ji. Anand Sahib is by Guru Amar Das Ji. Sukhmani Sahib is by Guru Arjan Dev Ji. Rehras Sahib and Kirtan Sohila gather verses of several Gurus. Jaap Sahib, Tav-Prasad Savaiye and Chaupai Sahib are in the Dasam Granth and are attributed to Guru Gobind Singh Ji in the Sikh tradition. See the page for each bani.
