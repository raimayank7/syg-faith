Jaap Sahib

A long hymn of the names and attributes of the Divine, recited in the morning Nitnem.

About Jaap Sahib

Jaap Sahib is a long composition made up of names and attributes of the Divine. In the Sikh tradition it is attributed to Guru Gobind Singh Ji, the tenth Guru, and it is found in the Dasam Granth, the collection of writings that is traditionally linked with him. It is not part of the Guru Granth Sahib. Scholars discuss the authorship of various parts of the Dasam Granth; Jaap Sahib is, however, one of the banis that the Sikh Rehat Maryada includes in the Nitnem.

The language is a mixture of Sanskrit, Braj and Persian words, and many verses are in a quick, rhythmic metre that makes it well suited to recitation. Because of the vocabulary many people find it harder to read than Japji Sahib; reading it with a teacher or a gutka with word meanings is helpful.

When it is recited

Jaap Sahib follows Japji Sahib in the morning Nitnem. A full reading takes about 25 to 35 minutes. It is also recited at some Khalsa ceremonies.

Structure

The bani has 199 numbered verses in the database (808 lines in 48 parts), in a series of metres (chhands) that change as the hymn goes on.
