Bhagavad Gita 16.21

Chapter 16, verse 21: the Sanskrit, the Roman letters, the meaning and what it is used for.

16.21

त्रिविधं नरकस्येदं द्वारं नाशनमात्मनः
कामः क्रोधस्तथा लोभस्तस्मादेतत्त्रयं त्यजेत्

trividhaṃ narakasyedaṃ dvāraṃ nāśanamātmanaḥ
kāmaḥ krodhastathā lobhastasmādetattrayaṃ tyajet

Meaning

Threefold is this gate of hell, destructive of the self: desire, anger and greed. Therefore one should give up these three.

Explanation

The same three, kama, krodha and lobha, are named in other places. The verse is plain: these are the forces which most reliably ruin people. It does not say that wanting or caring is wrong; it speaks of the three in their excess.

Where it fits

This is verse 21 of 24 in chapter 16, "Divine and demonic qualities" (Daivasura Sampad Vibhaga Yoga). previous verse (16.20) · next verse (16.22)
