The sixteen samskaras

The life ceremonies of Hindu tradition, from before birth to the last rites, in plain words.

A samskara is a ceremony that marks a stage of life. Choose a ceremony below, or read the full list.

About the samskaras

The word samskara means refinement or preparation. In Hindu tradition a samskara is a rite that marks a stage of a person’s life and is meant to bring the blessing of the family, the community and the gods to it. Ancient texts count sixteen, from the rites before birth to the funeral. Not every family performs all of them today. In many homes the best-known ones are the naming ceremony, the first feeding of solid food, the first haircut (mundan), the sacred thread (upanayana), marriage and the last rites; others have become family customs or are kept in a short form.

Each page below tells when the ceremony is traditionally done, what happens, what the common modern practice is and what questions people ask. Practice differs a great deal between regions, communities and families, and between those who follow older texts and those who follow family custom. Where this guide describes a ritual it describes what is commonly done and does not tell you what you must do; for your own family the elders and your priest are the guide.

Before and at birth

  • Garbhadhana (गर्भाधान)
  • Pumsavana (पुंसवन)
  • Simantonnayana (godh bharai) (सीमन्तोन्नयन)
  • Jatakarma (जातकर्म)

Childhood

  • Namakarana (naming ceremony) (नामकरण)
  • Nishkramana (निष्क्रमण)
  • Annaprashana (अन्नप्राशन)
  • Chudakarana (mundan) (चूडाकरण)
  • Karnavedha (कर्णवेध)
  • Vidyarambha (aksharabhyasa) (विद्यारम्भ)

Education and adulthood

  • Upanayana (janeu or sacred thread) (उपनयन)
  • Vedarambha (वेदारम्भ)
  • Keshanta (godana) (केशान्त)
  • Samavartana (समावर्तन)
  • Vivaha (wedding) (विवाह)

Death and ancestors

  • Antyeshti (last rites) (अन्त्येष्टि)
  • Pitru Paksha and shraddh
