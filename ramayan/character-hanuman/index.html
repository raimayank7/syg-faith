Hanuman

Hanuman: who Hanuman is in the Ramayana and the role in the story, in plain words.

The monkey hero, son of the wind god, whose devotion to Rama is the model of selfless service. He leaps across the sea, finds Sita, burns Lanka and carries the Sanjivani mountain. See Hanuman.

In the story

Hanuman is a monkey, son of the wind god Vayu and of Anjana, and a minister of the monkey king Sugriva. He meets Rama and Lakshmana near the mountain Rishyamukha and brings them to Sugriva. When the monkeys search for Sita it is Hanuman who leaps across the sea, meets Sita in the Ashoka grove, burns part of Lanka with a flame lit on his tail, and returns with news. In the war he carries a whole mountain of healing herbs to save Lakshmana.

How he is seen

Hanuman is the symbol of strength joined to devotion and humility. He is worshipped all over India, especially on Tuesdays and Saturdays, and the Hanuman Chalisa is among the most recited prayers. Wrestlers and people who need courage turn to him. Temples to him are often small and found at street corners.

Tellings

Stories of his childhood, such as mistaking the sun for a fruit and trying to eat it, come from later traditions and are not all in Valmiki. In some tellings he remains unmarried and celibate; in others, such as some southern traditions, he has a son.
