The Ramayana

The story of Rama, Sita and Hanuman, retold book by book, with the characters and the tellings.

Start with the story in seven books, then read the books one by one.

How to read this section

The Ramayana is told in many languages and many versions. This section follows the story of Valmiki’s Sanskrit epic in outline and mentions where the Ramcharitmanas of Tulsidas and other tellings differ. If you want the whole story in a short time, read the Ramayana in seven books, which summarises each book in a few lines, then read the books one by one, in order: the childhood and marriage of Rama, his exile, the abduction of Sita, the search, Hanuman’s journey to Lanka, the war and the return. If you prefer to start with people, the characters pages give a portrait of seventeen figures and what they do in the story.

The retelling is in our own words and quotes no verse. It is not a translation and has not been read by a scholar, and it takes no side where tellings differ; it says that they do. The festival page explains how Ram Navami, Dussehra and Diwali connect to the story.

The seven books

  • Bala Kanda: The book of childhood
  • Ayodhya Kanda: The book of Ayodhya
  • Aranya Kanda: The book of the forest
  • Kishkindha Kanda: The book of Kishkindha
  • Sundara Kanda: The beautiful book
  • Yuddha Kanda (Lanka Kanda): The book of the war
  • Uttara Kanda: The last book
