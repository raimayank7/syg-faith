Sita

Sita: who Sita is in the Ramayana and the role in the story, in plain words.

The daughter of the earth and wife of Rama, found by King Janaka in a furrow. She chose to share Rama's exile in the forest and stayed firm during her captivity in Lanka, and is remembered for her courage and fidelity. Tellers differ on how she is treated after the war; see the Yuddha and Uttara kandas.

In the story

Sita is found as a baby in a furrow by King Janaka of Mithila, and is raised as his daughter. She marries Rama after he strings the great bow of Shiva at her swayamvara. When Rama is exiled she chooses to go with him, saying her place is by her husband. In the forest Ravana takes her to Lanka by a trick, using the golden deer and the departure of the two brothers. She spends her captivity in the Ashoka grove, guarded by demonesses, refusing every offer. Hanuman finds her and gives her Rama's ring. After the war she is brought back; in many tellings she proves her purity by fire.

How she is seen

Sita is admired for her courage, patience and dignity. She is the model of a faithful wife for many, and for others the symbol of a woman who endures injustice. In modern retellings and in folk songs sung by women she is often given her own voice, speaking back to the men who decide what happens to her.

Where tellings differ

The scene of her trial by fire, and the later story of her being sent away and returning to the earth, are told differently in different versions, and some writers question them. Folk and regional Ramayanas, including women's songs, give her a much larger role than the Sanskrit epic.
