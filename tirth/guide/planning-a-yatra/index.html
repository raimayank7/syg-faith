Planning a yatra

How to plan a yatra in India: choosing the season, booking trains and stays through official sources, travelling with the elderly, what to carry and how to avoid touts and fraud.

A pilgrimage works best when it is planned a little in advance, especially if parents or older relatives are going. Here is a practical order.

1. Choose the season

Most shrines are best visited in the cooler months, from October to March. The Himalayan shrines are the exception: they open in spring and close with the snow, so the visit falls between May and October. Avoid the monsoon in the hills, and in the plains avoid the peak of summer. For festival times such as Shivaratri, Kumbh or Navratri, expect very large crowds.

2. Check the official site first

Every major temple has an official website or a trust, and the state tourism boards publish yatra pages. Check these for dress rules, darshan timings, booking and the special arrangements for the elderly. Timings and rules change, so do not rely on an old article, including ours.

3. Book trains, buses and stays early

Trains to the pilgrimage towns fill months ahead; Indian Railways opens bookings well in advance, and the official IRCTC site is the one to use. For stays, prefer the trust's own guest houses, government tourism hotels or reviewed hotels, and be careful of anyone who calls offering a booking.

4. Think about everyone in the group

Ask whether the route involves long walks, steps, a trek or altitude, and whether the temple offers a lift, a palanquin or a wheelchair. Take the doctor's advice for anyone with a heart, lung or knee problem, and carry the medicines for the whole trip with a list.

5. What to carry

A light bag, a water bottle, comfortable shoes that are easy to take off, a photocopy of identity cards, some cash in small notes, a power bank, a light rain cover, and clothes that meet the dress rules of the temple. Leave valuables at home.

6. Beware of fraud

Common frauds are fake booking websites, "VIP darshan" sold by agents and priests who demand large sums. Use the official booking site, pay only through it, and report fraud on the national helpline for cybercrime (1930) or at cybercrime.gov.in. See our notes on etiquette too.

Temple rules, opening seasons, booking arrangements and routes change. Before you travel, check the official website of the temple or the state tourism board, and be careful of anyone who asks for money to book darshan or a stay by phone or message.
