The Sapta Puri

Seven sacred cities that are said to give liberation.

A Sanskrit verse names seven cities as the ones that grant moksha, liberation: Ayodhya, Mathura, Maya (Haridwar), Kashi (Varanasi), Kanchi, Avantika (Ujjain) and Dvaravati (Dwarka). They are spread over the whole of India and belong to different traditions: Rama, Krishna, Shiva, Vishnu and the river Ganga each have their cities.

CityStateWhat makes it sacred
AyodhyaUttar PradeshThe birthplace of Rama, on the bank of the Sarayu.
MathuraUttar PradeshThe birthplace of Krishna, on the Yamuna.
HaridwarUttarakhandThe place where the Ganga leaves the mountains for the plains; the site of the Kumbh Mela.
Varanasi (Kashi)Uttar PradeshThe city of Shiva on the Ganga, and a place for the last rites.
KanchipuramTamil NaduThe "city of a thousand temples", with shrines of both Shiva and Vishnu.
UjjainMadhya PradeshThe city of Mahakaleshwar on the Shipra, and a place of the Kumbh Mela.
DwarkaGujaratThe city of Krishna on the western coast.

Several of them also hold a Kumbh Mela: Haridwar and Ujjain, together with Prayagraj and Nashik.

How the list is used

The verse that names the seven cities is a list for remembering, and different traditions give it different weight. Followers of Vishnu stress Ayodhya, Mathura and Dwarka; followers of Shiva stress Kashi and Ujjain; Kanchi is known for both Shiva and Vishnu temples and for learning. The idea behind the list is that a pilgrim who visits a city that is holy to a particular tradition receives its blessing, and that the seven together cover the whole country, from Dwarka in the west to Ayodhya and Varanasi in the north and Kanchipuram in the south. Many people visit the cities one at a time over several years rather than in one journey.

Visiting

These are crowded cities, and the best seasons for them are the cooler months. Check the opening hours and any dress rules on the official page of each temple, book travel in advance around the big festivals, and prefer official sources to touts who offer to arrange the rites. Our pilgrimage planning guide gives general advice.

Temple rules, opening seasons, booking arrangements and routes change. Before you travel, check the official website of the temple or the state tourism board, and be careful of anyone who asks for money to book darshan or a stay by phone or message.
