Rahu Kaal on Monday

Which part of the day it is, and the time in 30 large cities through the year.

Monday belongs to the Moon (Soma). Rahu Kaal is the second part of the daylight, early in the morning, soon after most people start their day, which is why Monday is the weekday where it most often catches shops and offices opening.

The rule is the same in every place: cut the time from sunrise to sunset into eight equal parts and take part number 2. On a day that runs exactly from 6 am to 6 pm each part is 90 minutes, so the textbook answer for Monday is 7:30 am to 9:00 am. Real days are never exactly 12 hours, and sunrise in Delhi is not sunrise in Chennai, so the table below gives the times in the 30 largest Indian cities for the middle of January, April, July and October 2027.

Cities in the north and west, where days change more through the year, move the most between the columns; cities near the equator hardly move. Open a city for its full weekday table or for today's time, or see how the times are calculated.

How much the time moves

Across the 30 cities and four dates in the table, Rahu Kaal on Monday starts as early as 6:41 am (Kolkata, 15 July) and as late as 8:51 am (Rajkot, 15 January), a spread of 129 minutes. The spread comes from two things: cities in the east see the sun rise earlier than cities in the west, and cities far from the equator have days that are much longer in summer and shorter in winter. If you are planning something around Rahu Kaal on a Monday, do not use a time from another city or another season; use the page of your own city, which gives the time for every weekday through the year.

What people do about it

Those who follow Rahu Kaal avoid starting a new journey, signing a contract, buying something important or beginning a ceremony during it; work already in progress can usually go on. Many people do not follow it at all, and in some regions it is not a custom. It is a matter of belief and family practice and has no scientific basis, so we give the times for those who want them and take no position on whether they matter.
