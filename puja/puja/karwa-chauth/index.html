Karwa Chauth Vrat and Puja (करवा चौथ)

Karwa Chauth is a fast kept by married women in north India for the well-being of their husbands. It ends with the sight of the moon.. Items needed and the steps, in plain words.

Who is worshipped and when

The puja is for Shiva-Parvati, Ganesha and the Moon. It is done: The fourth day after the full moon in the month of Kartik (October or November).

Items you need (samagri)

Tick the items as you collect them. The first few are specific to this puja; the rest are the basic items for any puja. You can print this page as a checklist.

Steps

  1. Before sunrise, eat the sargi, the meal that the mother-in-law traditionally gives.
  2. Keep the fast through the day. Many keep it without water; those who are unwell, pregnant or elderly may take advice and modify it.
  3. In the evening, women dress and sit together for the katha, with the karwa and the lamp.
  4. After moonrise, see the moon through the sieve, offer water, and then see the husband's face.
  5. The husband gives the first sip of water and the first bite to break the fast.

Notes

Moonrise differs from city to city; use the moonrise time for your own city. Some couples now keep the fast together. The fast is a personal choice and should not be at the cost of health.

Questions people ask

When is Karwa Chauth Vrat and Puja done?

The fourth day after the full moon in the month of Kartik (October or November). Check a panchang or ask a priest for the exact time in the current year.

Can it be done at home?

Yes. This outline is for a simple home puja. For a long ceremony, a priest is usually asked to lead it.
