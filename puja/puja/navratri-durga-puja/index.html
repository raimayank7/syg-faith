Navratri and Durga Puja (नवरात्रि दुर्गा पूजा)

Navratri is a nine-night festival of the goddess Durga. At home many families set up a kalash and worship the goddess on all nine days, with fasting.. Items needed and the steps, in plain words.

Who is worshipped and when

The puja is for Durga in nine forms. It is done: The nine nights of Navratri (twice a year) and Durga Puja in Bengal.

Items you need (samagri)

Tick the items as you collect them. The first few are specific to this puja; the rest are the basic items for any puja. You can print this page as a checklist.

Steps

  1. On the first day, install the kalash (ghatasthapana) and sow the barley in a clean pot at the auspicious time given by the panchang.
  2. Every day, bathe and light the lamp, offer flowers and read or recite the Durga Saptashati or a simpler hymn.
  3. Each day is linked with one form of the goddess (Shailaputri, Brahmacharini, Chandraghanta and so on); read about them on our Durga page.
  4. On the eighth or ninth day, many families do Kanya Pujan, worshipping young girls as forms of the goddess, with food and gifts.
  5. On the tenth day, Vijayadashami, the kalash is closed and the barley sprouts are distributed.

Notes

Fasting is a personal choice, and the food of the fast (no grains, onion or garlic in many families) is a matter of family custom. Anyone with a health condition should adjust the fast.

Questions people ask

When is Navratri and Durga Puja done?

The nine nights of Navratri (twice a year) and Durga Puja in Bengal. Check a panchang or ask a priest for the exact time in the current year.

Can it be done at home?

Yes. This outline is for a simple home puja. For a long ceremony, a priest is usually asked to lead it.

This page describes common practice in plain words. Families, regions and priests differ in the details, and your own family tradition or your priest comes first. Dates and muhurat (auspicious times) change every year: ask a priest or use a trusted panchang. Nothing here promises any result.
