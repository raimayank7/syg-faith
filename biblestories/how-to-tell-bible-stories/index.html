How to tell Bible stories to children

Practical tips for telling Bible stories to children: choosing the story, keeping it short, using questions and actions, handling hard parts, and linking it to everyday life.

Children remember stories that are told simply, with a voice and a face, more than stories that are read. A few habits help.

  • Choose by age. Under six: short stories with a clear picture, such as Noah, David and Jonah. Seven to eleven: stories with more people and a longer path, such as Joseph and Ruth.
  • Keep it short. Five to ten minutes is enough.
  • Use your voice and hands. Make the storm, the lion and the whisper.
  • Ask questions and listen to the answers; children often see something you did not.
  • Handle the hard parts truthfully and gently. The Bible includes violence and death. Tell it at the child's level without hiding it, and answer questions honestly.
  • Link the story to their day: sharing, saying sorry, being brave, waiting.
  • Read from the Bible too for older children, and let them find the passage.

The stories are simple retellings for children and are not a substitute for reading the Bible. Churches teach the stories in different ways; adapt to your own tradition.
