Meditations by topic

Passages gathered by theme.

People rarely read the Meditations from the first page to the last; they come with a question. How do I deal with someone who keeps annoying me? How do I think about death? How do I start the day when I do not want to? The six topic pages gather the passages that speak to those questions, each with a short note in plain words saying what the emperor is getting at.

Read a topic page the way Marcus Aurelius wrote: one passage at a time, and slowly. Take one thought away for the day rather than all of them. If a passage is hard, read the note beside it and then go back to the passage. The notes are our own wording and are not a scholar’s commentary. Many of the ideas come from the Stoic school he followed, which taught that we cannot control what happens but can control how we judge and answer it; that idea runs through all six topics.

Six themes gather the passages that people look up most often.

  • The morning: starting the day
  • Anger and other people's faults
  • Death and the shortness of life
  • Opinion, judgement and what disturbs us
  • Calm of mind
  • Duty and work

The text is George Long's English translation of 1862, in the public domain, and its old-fashioned English ("thou", "thy") is unchanged. The notes beside it are our own wording. Marcus Aurelius wrote these for himself; they are a diary, not a book of rules.
