Rama (राम)

The ideal man, avatar of Vishnu: who Rama is, the stories, the symbols, how Rama is worshipped and the festivals.

Who is Rama?

Rama is the seventh avatar of Vishnu and the hero of the Ramayana. He is known as Maryada Purushottam, the ideal man who kept to dharma in each role: son, brother, husband and king. The Ramayana of Valmiki, and in north India the Ramcharitmanas of Tulsidas, tell his story. For many Hindus the name Rama is itself a prayer.

Also known as
Raghunath, Ramachandra, Maryada Purushottam
Family
Son of King Dasharatha and Queen Kausalya of Ayodhya; husband of Sita; brothers Lakshmana, Bharata and Shatrughna.
Symbols
A bow and arrow, a dark blue-green skin, crown and a gentle face, often with Sita, Lakshmana and Hanuman
Vehicle
None
Known as
The ideal man, avatar of Vishnu

Stories

He was exiled for fourteen years because of the wish of his stepmother Kaikeyi, and went to the forest with Sita and Lakshmana. Ravana, the king of Lanka, carried Sita away; Rama, with the help of Hanuman and an army of monkeys and bears, built a bridge to Lanka and defeated Ravana. His return to Ayodhya is celebrated as Diwali in many parts of India.

Worship

Many families greet each other with "Ram Ram" or "Jai Shri Ram". The recitation of the Ramcharitmanas is a common practice. Tuesday and Saturday are linked with Rama's devotee Hanuman.

Festivals

Rama Navami (in March or April) is his birthday; Dussehra celebrates his victory over Ravana and Diwali his return to Ayodhya.
