Parvati (पार्वती)

The mother goddess, wife of Shiva: who Parvati is, the stories, the symbols, how Parvati is worshipped and the festivals.

Who is Parvati?

Parvati, "daughter of the mountain", is the gentle form of the divine feminine and the shakti, the power, of Shiva. She is shown as the devoted wife and mother, but also as a woman who won Shiva by her own austerity. She is seen by her devotees as the one without whom Shiva is not complete: it is said that Shiva without Shakti is only a corpse (shava).

Also known as
Uma, Gauri, Shakti, Durga (in one form)
Family
Wife of Shiva; mother of Ganesha and Kartikeya; daughter of Himavan, the king of the mountains.
Symbols
A lotus, a mirror, often shown seated beside Shiva with her children; as Gauri, a golden fair form
Vehicle
A lion in her fierce forms
Known as
The mother goddess, wife of Shiva

Stories

In an earlier life she was Sati, who gave up her body when her father insulted Shiva. She was reborn as Parvati and performed severe penance to win Shiva back. The god of love, Kama, tried to bring Shiva out of his meditation and was burnt by his third eye; but Parvati's own devotion at last won him. Her sons Ganesha and Kartikeya come from her story.

Worship

Women keep fasts for Gauri and Parvati for the happiness of the family; unmarried girls pray to her for a good husband, and married women for the long life of their husbands. Fridays and Mondays are both used.

Festivals

Teej and Gauri Tritiya are festivals especially for her, and she is part of Navratri as one of the nine forms of Durga. Gangaur in Rajasthan is a spring festival in which women worship her.
