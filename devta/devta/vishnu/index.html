Vishnu (विष्णु)

The preserver: who Vishnu is, the stories, the symbols, how Vishnu is worshipped and the festivals.

Who is Vishnu?

In the Trimurti, Vishnu is the preserver of the world. He comes in a human or other form when righteousness declines, and these descents are called avatars; Rama and Krishna are his best-loved ones. Vaishnavas, the followers of Vishnu, see him as the supreme god. He is shown lying on the serpent Shesha in the ocean of milk with Lakshmi massaging his feet.

Also known as
Narayana, Hari, Madhava, Jagannath
Family
Husband of Lakshmi; Brahma is born from his navel lotus in some stories. His home is Vaikuntha.
Symbols
Conch (shankha), discus (Sudarshana chakra), mace (gada) and lotus (padma); blue skin; yellow robes
Vehicle
The eagle Garuda; he rests on the serpent Shesha
Known as
The preserver

Stories

When the earth was drowned, he took the form of a boar and lifted it on his tusks. When the demon Hiranyakashipu could not be killed by day or night, in or out of a house, by man or beast, he came as Narasimha, half man and half lion, at twilight at the threshold. When King Bali had conquered the three worlds, he came as the dwarf Vamana and asked for three steps of land, and then spanned the universe in two. These and other forms are described on our page on the ten avatars.

Worship

Thursday is the traditional day for Vishnu for many families (some say Saturday or Ekadashi). The tulsi plant is his favourite leaf and yellow his colour. The Vishnu Sahasranama, the thousand names of Vishnu, is one of the most recited texts.

Festivals

Ekadashi, the eleventh day of each fortnight, is observed with fasting by many Vaishnavas. Vaikuntha Ekadashi, Rama Navami, Krishna Janmashtami and the Narasimha Jayanti are the best known festivals of his avatars.

Related

  • Narasimha: The man-lion avatar of Vishnu
