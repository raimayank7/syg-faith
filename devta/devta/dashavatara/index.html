The ten avatars of Vishnu

Dashavatara: the ten descents of Vishnu, from the fish to Kalki, and the stories behind them.

An avatar is a descent: the form that Vishnu, the preserver, takes when the world is in danger and righteousness has declined. The most widely told list, called the Dashavatara, has ten. The first four are from the earliest age, then come the half-human and the human forms. The lists vary a little between traditions, as noted below.

  1. Matsya, the fish. When a great flood was to cover the earth, Vishnu came as a fish and led the boat of Manu, who carried the seeds of all life and the Vedas, to safety.
  2. Kurma, the tortoise. When the gods and demons churned the ocean of milk, the mountain Mandara they used as a churning rod began to sink; Vishnu became a tortoise and held it on his back.
  3. Varaha, the boar. The demon Hiranyaksha took the earth and hid it under the primordial waters; Vishnu as a boar dived down, killed him and lifted the earth on his tusks.
  4. Narasimha, the man-lion. To protect his devotee Prahlada, Vishnu appeared from a pillar as a being who was neither man nor beast and killed the demon king Hiranyakashipu at twilight, on a threshold.
  5. Vamana, the dwarf. King Bali had won the three worlds. Vishnu came as a small brahmin boy and asked for as much land as he could cover in three steps; he grew to cover the earth and the sky in two, and placed his third step on Bali\'s head.
  6. Parashurama, Rama with the axe. A sage born in a brahmin family who took up the axe given to him by Shiva, and is said to have cleared the earth of oppressive kings; in some traditions he is still alive.
  7. Rama, the prince of Ayodhya. The hero of the Ramayana, the ideal son, husband and king. See Rama.
  8. Krishna, the cowherd and charioteer. The teacher of the Bhagavad Gita and the beloved child and friend of Vrindavan. See Krishna.
  9. Buddha, the awakened one. In many Puranic lists the ninth avatar is the Buddha. Some lists, especially in Vaishnava traditions of the south and in the Bhagavata Purana in some readings, name Balarama, the elder brother of Krishna, in this place, and the lists differ.
  10. Kalki, the one yet to come. The final avatar, who is expected at the end of the present age, the Kali Yuga, riding a white horse with a sword, to end injustice and begin a new age.

The order of the first nine is often read as a picture of the development of life, from water to land and from animals to humans, but this is a modern reading of a traditional list and is not part of the Puranic text. Read more about Vishnu and about Narasimha.

These pages describe traditions as they are told in the Puranas and in popular devotion. Practices differ between regions, communities and families, and your own family\'s custom comes first. This is not religious instruction.
