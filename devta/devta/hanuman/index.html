Hanuman (हनुमान)

Devotee of Rama, god of strength and courage: who Hanuman is, the stories, the symbols, how Hanuman is worshipped and the festivals.

Who is Hanuman?

Hanuman is the greatest devotee of Rama and the hero of the Sundara Kanda of the Ramayana. He is worshipped as the god of strength, courage and selfless service. He is also the god of wrestlers, and many akharas keep his image.

Also known as
Bajrangbali, Maruti, Anjaneya, Pavanputra
Family
Son of Anjana and Kesari, and called the son of the wind god Vayu by his blessing.
Symbols
A mace (gada), a mountain in his hand (the Sanjivani hill), a red face and body, often shown tearing open his chest to show Rama and Sita in his heart
Vehicle
He needs none; he flies
Known as
Devotee of Rama, god of strength and courage

Stories

As a child he mistook the sun for a fruit and leapt into the sky to eat it, which led the gods to give him boons. In the Ramayana he leaps across the sea to Lanka and finds Sita, sets Lanka on fire with his burning tail and later carries the mountain with the Sanjivani herb to save Lakshmana. The Hanuman Chalisa, 40 verses composed by Tulsidas, is the most recited praise of him.

Worship

Tuesday and Saturday are his days; devotees apply sindoor mixed with oil, offer red flowers, bananas and laddus, and read the Hanuman Chalisa. Many do not eat certain foods on those days. His temples are the most common in the villages of north India.

Festivals

Hanuman Jayanti (in March or April) is his birth anniversary. Tuesdays and Saturdays are kept all through the year.
