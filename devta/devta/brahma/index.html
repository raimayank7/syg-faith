Brahma (ब्रह्मा)

The creator: who Brahma is, the stories, the symbols, how Brahma is worshipped and the festivals.

Who is Brahma?

In the Trimurti Brahma is the creator. Despite this he has few temples; the best known in India is at Pushkar in Rajasthan. He is shown with four faces looking in four directions, which stand for the four Vedas and the four directions, and with a beard that indicates age and wisdom.

Also known as
Prajapati, Svayambhu, Pitamaha
Family
Husband of Saraswati; in some stories born from the lotus that rose from Vishnu's navel.
Symbols
Four faces, four arms holding the Vedas, a rosary, a water pot and a lotus; a white beard
Vehicle
The swan (hamsa)
Known as
The creator

Stories

The Puranas tell how Brahma woke from the lotus on Vishnu's navel and created the world. In a story widely told, he and Vishnu once argued about who was greater, and Shiva appeared as a pillar of fire whose top and bottom neither could find; Brahma lied that he had found the top, and for this was cursed that he would not be worshipped as the others are.

Worship

There is little daily worship of Brahma; many Hindus see him in the creative power present in all things. Pilgrims visit Pushkar, especially at the time of the Kartik Purnima fair.

Festivals

The Pushkar fair on Kartik Purnima (in October or November) is the one great festival linked to Brahma.
