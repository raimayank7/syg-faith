Saraswati (सरस्वती)

Goddess of knowledge, music and the arts: who Saraswati is, the stories, the symbols, how Saraswati is worshipped and the festivals.

Who is Saraswati?

Saraswati is the goddess of speech, learning, music and the arts. She is shown in white, which stands for purity of knowledge, and holds a veena in her hands. In the oldest hymns the name belongs to a river, and the river and the goddess of speech were joined together over time. Students, teachers, musicians and writers pray to her.

Also known as
Sharada, Vagdevi, Veena-vadini
Family
Consort of Brahma in the Puranas; in some traditions the daughter of Shiva and Parvati.
Symbols
The veena, a book (the Vedas), a rosary of crystal beads, a pot of water; white clothes; seated on a white lotus
Vehicle
A swan; sometimes a peacock
Known as
Goddess of knowledge, music and the arts

Stories

In some stories Brahma created her from his mind, or she came from his mouth; in others she is born of Shiva and Parvati. The swan with her is said to separate milk from water, which stands for the ability to tell the true from the false. Her peacock stands for the pride that is controlled.

Worship

No single weekday is fixed for her; children begin writing (Vidyarambham) with a prayer to her. Books and instruments are kept clean and are not touched with feet.

Festivals

Vasant Panchami (in January or February) is her great day, when students put their books before her image; in South India, the last days of Navratri are Saraswati Puja, when tools and books are worshipped.
