Ganesha (गणेश)

Remover of obstacles: who Ganesha is, the stories, the symbols, how Ganesha is worshipped and the festivals.

Who is Ganesha?

Ganesha is the god people remember first. Most prayers, weddings, business openings, school years and new homes in India begin with a thought of him, which is why he is called Vighnaharta, the remover of obstacles, and Vinayaka, the leader. His elephant head stands for wisdom and a large listening mind; his big belly for the ability to take in all of life's good and bad; his small mouse for the control of desire.

Also known as
Ganapati, Vinayaka, Vighnaharta, Ekadanta
Family
Son of Shiva and Parvati; brother of Kartikeya. Riddhi and Siddhi are named as his consorts in many traditions.
Symbols
Elephant head, one tusk, big belly, an axe (parashu), a noose (pasha), a broken tusk, a bowl of modak sweets
Vehicle
A small mouse (mushika)
Known as
Remover of obstacles

Stories

The best known story says that Parvati made a boy from the paste she used for her bath and set him to guard her door. When Shiva was refused entry, he cut off the boy's head in anger; to console Parvati he put on the boy the head of the first creature he found, an elephant. Another tradition tells how he wrote the Mahabharata as Vyasa dictated it, and broke his own tusk to use as a pen when his writing reed broke.

Worship

He is worshipped before any other god. The traditional weekday is Wednesday for many families, and Tuesday (Sankashti Chaturthi days) for others. The sweet most linked to him is the modak, and durva grass and red flowers are the common offerings. The word "Om gam Ganapataye namah" is his best known mantra name.

Festivals

Ganesh Chaturthi (in August or September) is his great festival, especially in Maharashtra, with clay images installed in homes and neighbourhoods and immersed after several days. Sankashti Chaturthi comes every month.
