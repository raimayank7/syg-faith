Shani (शनि)

The planet Saturn and the god of justice: who Shani is, the stories, the symbols, how Shani is worshipped and the festivals.

Who is Shani?

Shani is Saturn, one of the Navagraha, and is also thought of as a god who gives each person what they have earned. He is often feared, but his devotees say that he is just and rewards honest work and patience. The period of seven and a half years called sade sati is linked in popular belief with his transit, and is a time that people approach with care.

Also known as
Shanaishchara, Chhayaputra
Family
Son of Surya and Chhaya; brother of Yama.
Symbols
A dark body, a crow or vulture, a sword, a bow or a trident; an iron colour
Vehicle
A crow; in some pictures a vulture or a buffalo
Known as
The planet Saturn and the god of justice

Stories

In stories he is shown as a god whose look brings trouble, and as a devotee of Shiva and Krishna. In a popular story, King Vikramaditya was brought low by Shani and later praised him. Shingnapur in Maharashtra is famed for its temple without doors where he is worshipped as a presence in a black stone.

Worship

Saturday is his day. Devotees offer mustard oil, black sesame, iron and black cloth, and feed the poor. Hanuman is also worshipped on Saturday as a protector. Please note that astrological beliefs about Shani are matters of faith and not of science.

Festivals

Shani Jayanti (in May or June) and Shani Amavasya are the days for him.
