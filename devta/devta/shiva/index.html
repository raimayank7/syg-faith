Shiva (शिव)

The destroyer and the great yogi: who Shiva is, the stories, the symbols, how Shiva is worshipped and the festivals.

Who is Shiva?

In the Trimurti of Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva, Shiva is the one who dissolves what has run its course, so that the cycle can begin again. For his devotees, the Shaivas, he is the supreme god. He is shown as a yogi seated in meditation, as the dancer Nataraja whose dance sustains the cosmos, and as the gentle householder with a family. He is worshipped in the form of the lingam, a rounded pillar that stands for the formless.

Also known as
Mahadev, Shankar, Bholenath, Nataraja, Rudra
Family
Husband of Parvati; father of Ganesha and Kartikeya. His home is Mount Kailash.
Symbols
Crescent moon on the head, the Ganga in his matted hair, a third eye, a serpent around the neck, a trident (trishula), a drum (damaru), ash on the body
Vehicle
The bull Nandi
Known as
The destroyer and the great yogi

Stories

In the churning of the ocean of milk by gods and demons, a poison called halahala came up that threatened all creation; Shiva drank it and held it in his throat, which turned blue, and so he is called Neelkanth. Another story tells how the river Ganga fell from heaven and Shiva caught her in his hair so that her force would not break the earth. His marriage to Parvati, who did great penance to win him, is told in the Puranas and sung in many local songs.

Worship

Monday is his traditional day. Devotees pour water or milk on the lingam and offer bilva (bel) leaves, which are the offering most linked with him; the mantra "Om namah Shivaya" is the best known of all. In some parts of India Shiva is the god of a particular local temple; the twelve jyotirlingas are among the best known of his shrines.

Festivals

Maha Shivaratri (in February or March) is his great night, with fasting and an all-night vigil. The month of Shravan (July and August) is also a special month for him, with Mondays kept as fasting days by many.
