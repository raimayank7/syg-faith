Symbols and vehicles

The vahana (vehicle) and the symbols of each deity, and what they are said to mean.

Every god and goddess in Hindu art is shown with a particular vehicle (vahana) and with particular objects in the hands or on the body. These are not decoration: they are a kind of shorthand that tells a devotee who is being shown and what that deity stands for. The same symbol can carry more than one meaning in different traditions.

Some common symbols

  • The lotus stands for purity: it rises from mud and is not stained by it.
  • The conch (shankha) is the sound of creation, the first sound; Vishnu holds a conch.
  • The discus (chakra) is time and the cycle of the world, and also the weapon that cuts through evil.
  • The trident (trishula) of Shiva is often said to stand for the three qualities (gunas) or the three times, past, present and future.
  • The vehicle of a deity is often something that the deity is shown to control: the mouse of Ganesha stands for desire, the lion of Durga for power under control.
  • The third eye of Shiva is the eye of knowledge, which burns away illusion.

These pages describe traditions as they are told in the Puranas and in popular devotion. Practices differ between regions, communities and families, and your own family's custom comes first. This is not religious instruction.
