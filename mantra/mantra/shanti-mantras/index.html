Shanti mantras

Prayers for peace and good learning, as they appear at the end of the Ganapati Atharvashirsha.

ॐ सहनाववतु ।
सहनौभुनक्तु ॥
सह वीर्यं करवावहै ।
तेजस्विनावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै ॥
ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः ॥

oṃ sahanāvavatu |
sahanaubhunaktu ||
saha vīryaṃ karavāvahai |
tejasvināvadhītamastu mā vidviṣāvahai ||
oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ||

In plain words: May he protect us both together; may he nourish us both together; may we work together with great energy; may our study be illuminating; may we not hate each other. Om, peace, peace, peace. It is a prayer of teacher and student at the start of study.

ॐ भद्रं कर्णेभिः शृणुयाम देवाः ।
भद्रं पश्येमाक्षभिर्यजत्राः ॥
स्थिरैरङ्गैस्तुष्टुवांसस्तनूभिः ।
व्यशेम देवहितं यदायुः ॥

oṃ bhadraṃ karṇebhiḥ śṛṇuyāma devāḥ |
bhadraṃ paśyemākṣabhiryajatrāḥ ||
sthirairaṅgaistuṣṭuvāṃsastanūbhiḥ |
vyaśema devahitaṃ yadāyuḥ ||

In plain words: May we hear what is good with our ears, O gods; may we see what is good with our eyes, O worthy of worship; praising with firm limbs and bodies, may we live the life that is given by the gods.

ॐ स्वस्ति न इन्द्रो वृद्धश्रवाः ।
स्वस्ति नः पूषा विश्ववेदाः ॥
स्वस्तिनस्तार्क्ष्यो अरिष्टनेमिः ।
स्वस्ति नो बृहस्पतिर्दधातु ॥

oṃ svasti na indro vṛddhaśravāḥ |
svasti naḥ pūṣā viśvavedāḥ ||
svastinastārkṣyo ariṣṭanemiḥ |
svasti no bṛhaspatirdadhātu ||

In plain words: May Indra of great renown bring us well-being; may Pushan, the all-knowing, bring us well-being; may Tarkshya (the eagle) of unharmed wheel bring us well-being; may Brihaspati grant us well-being.

The three peaces

The closing "Om shantih shantih shantih" is said three times. A common explanation is that the first is for peace from the forces of nature, the second from other beings and the third from within oneself. The Sanskrit comes from the Atharva-Veda tradition; the two verses are found in several Upanishads. Here we give them as they appear on the page of the Ganapati Atharvashirsha.

Questions people ask

What is a shanti mantra?

A shanti mantra is a prayer for peace said at the start or end of a study, a ceremony or a text. "Om shanti shanti shanti" asks for peace in the three realms: in oneself, around oneself and from the divine.

The meanings and notes are our own words and not an authorised translation. Mantras belong to living traditions, and families and teachers differ in how they chant and use them; please ask yours. We make no claim that any mantra brings any result.
