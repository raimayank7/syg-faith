Mahamrityunjaya Mantra

Rigveda 7.59.12: the Sanskrit, the Roman letters, the words one by one and the meaning.

Rigveda 7.59.12

त्र्यम्बकं यजामहे सुगन्धिं पुष्टिवर्धनम् ।
उर्वारुकमिव बन्धनान्मृत्योर्मुक्षीय मामृतात् ॥

tryambakaṃ yajāmahe sugandhiṃ puṣṭivardhanam |
urvārukamiva bandhanānmṛtyormukṣīya māmṛtāt ||

Meaning

"We worship the three-eyed one (Tryambaka), who is fragrant and who nourishes; as a ripe cucumber is freed from its stem, may I be freed from death, and not from immortality." The words: tryambakam the three-eyed (or "father of the three"), a name of Rudra, later Shiva; yajāmahe we worship; sugandhim the fragrant; puṣṭivardhanam the one who increases nourishment; urvārukam iva like a cucumber; bandhanāt from its stem or bond; mṛtyor mukṣīya may I be freed from death; mā amṛtāt not from immortality.

The image

A ripe cucumber falls from the vine by itself, painlessly, without being torn from it. The prayer asks for death in that way, a natural and untroubled release, and, in the second half, for freedom from the fear of death that leaves the deeper life untouched.

Use

The mantra is chanted by many families in times of illness and anxiety, and for the peace of the departed. Chanting it can bring calm to the person who chants and to those near, but it is a prayer, not a treatment: please get medical help for illness. The long form "Om hraum joom sah" and other additions found in some books are not part of the Rigveda verse.

Questions people ask

What does the Mahamrityunjaya Mantra mean?

We worship the three-eyed one, fragrant and the nourisher; like a ripe cucumber from its stem, may I be freed from death, not from immortality.

Where is it from?

Rigveda 7.59.12, addressed to Tryambaka, a name of Rudra (Shiva).
