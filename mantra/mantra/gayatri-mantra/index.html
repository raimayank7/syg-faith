Gayatri Mantra

Rigveda 3.62.10: the Sanskrit, the Roman letters, the words one by one and the meaning.

Rigveda 3.62.10

ॐ भूर्भुवः स्वः
तत्सवितुर्वरेण्यं भर्गो देवस्य धीमहि ।
धियो यो नः प्रचोदयात् ॥

oṃ bhūrbhuvaḥ svaḥ
tatsaviturvareṇyaṃ bhargo devasya dhīmahi |
dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt ||

Meaning

"We meditate on that most excellent radiance of the divine Savitar; may he inspire our thoughts." The words: tat that; savituḥ of Savitar, the sun as the one who stirs life and thought; vareṇyam most worthy of choice, excellent; bhargaḥ radiance, splendour; devasya of the divine, the shining one; dhīmahi we meditate on, we hold in mind; dhiyaḥ thoughts, understandings; yaḥ who; naḥ our; pracodayāt may he stimulate, impel.

The opening words

"Om bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ" are three words that name the earth, the space between earth and sky, and the heaven; they were added before the verse in later practice and are not part of the original Rigveda verse. The verse itself begins with tat savituḥ.

Metre and name

The mantra is in a metre called gāyatrī, with three lines of eight syllables; the name Gayatri comes from the metre. It is also addressed in tradition as a goddess, Gayatri. Ours is a reading of the verse; for the meaning in the philosophy of a particular school, ask a teacher.

Use

It is recited at dawn, noon and dusk in the daily sandhya of many Hindus, and many families teach it to children. It is chanted aloud or silently; counting on a rosary of 108 beads is a common practice. See how to chant.

Questions people ask

What does the Gayatri Mantra mean?

We meditate on the excellent radiance of the divine Savitar (the sun as the source of light); may he stimulate our thoughts.

Where is it from?

It is verse 10 of hymn 3.62 of the Rigveda, traditionally ascribed to the sage Vishvamitra.
