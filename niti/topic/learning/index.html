Learning and knowledge

Chanakya Niti on study, skill and wisdom

Learning gets high praise: it is a hidden treasure, a mother in a foreign land and the thing that makes beauty and birth worth anything. The verses also warn that learning fades without practice.

The verses

Chanakya Niti 1.16

विषाद् अप्य् अमृतं ग्राह्यम् अमेध्याद् अपि काञ्चनम् । नीचाद् अप्युत्तमां विद्या स्त्रीरत्नं दुष्कुलाद् अपि

viṣād apy amṛtaṃ grāhyam amedhyād api kāñcanam | nīcād apyuttamāṃ vidyā strīratnaṃ duṣkulād api

In plain words: Take nectar even from poison, gold even from filth and the best learning even from a humble person. (The last line of the verse is about marriage and reflects the society of its time.)

Chanakya Niti 3.8

रूपयौवनसम्पन्ना विशालकुलसम्भवाः । विद्याहीना न शोभन्ते निर्गन्धा इवकिशुकाः

rūpayauvanasampannā viśālakulasambhavāḥ | vidyāhīnā na śobhante nirgandhā ivakiśukāḥ

In plain words: People with beauty, youth and a noble family who have no learning do not shine, like the scentless kimshuka flower.

Chanakya Niti 3.13

कोऽतिभारः समर्थानां किं दूरं व्यवसायिनाम्। को विदेशः सुविद्यानां कः परः प्रियवादिनाम्

ko'tibhāraḥ samarthānāṃ kiṃ dūraṃ vyavasāyinām| ko videśaḥ suvidyānāṃ kaḥ paraḥ priyavādinām

In plain words: What is too heavy for the capable, too far for the hard-working, a foreign land for the learned, or a stranger for the sweet-spoken?

Chanakya Niti 4.5

कामधेनुगुण विद्या ह्यकाले फलदायिनी । प्रवासे मातृसदृशी विद्या गुप्तं धनं स्मृतम्

kāmadhenuguṇa vidyā hyakāle phaladāyinī | pravāse mātṛsadṛśī vidyā guptaṃ dhanaṃ smṛtam

In plain words: Learning is like the wish-granting cow: it gives its fruit at any time; in a foreign land it is like a mother; it is hidden wealth.

Chanakya Niti 5.8

अभ्यासाध्दार्यते विद्या कुलं शीलेन धार्यते । गुणेन ज्ञायते त्वार्यः कोपो नेत्रेण गम्यते

abhyāsādhdāryate vidyā kulaṃ śīlena dhāryate | guṇena jñāyate tvāryaḥ kopo netreṇa gamyate

In plain words: Learning is held by practice; a family by good conduct; a noble person is known by qualities; anger is known by the eyes.

The Sanskrit is from a volunteer-made online edition and may contain slips; the gists are our own plain summaries, not a translation. Maxims reflect the society and the views of their time, and some verses about women, birth and class are not views we share; we show the text as it is and do not comment on those verses.
