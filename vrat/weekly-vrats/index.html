Weekly vrats

The weekday vrats of Hindu tradition: Monday for Shiva, Tuesday for Hanuman, Wednesday for Ganesha, Thursday for Vishnu, Friday for the goddess and Saturday for Shani, with the usual practice.

Many families keep a weekly vrat. The deity of the day differs by region and family; the table gives the most common choices.

DayDeityCommon practice
SundaySuryaOne meal without salt in some families; offering water to the sun
MondayShivaFast until evening, bilva leaves and water on the lingam; 16 Mondays (Solah Somvar) is a popular vow
TuesdayHanuman, MangalaOne meal, reading the Hanuman Chalisa
WednesdayGanesha, Vishnu in some regionsGreen food, Ganesha puja
ThursdayVishnu, Brihaspati, Sai BabaYellow clothes and food, banana worship in some places
FridayLakshmi, Durga, Santoshi MataSweets, no sour food in the Santoshi Mata vrat
SaturdayShani, HanumanOil lamp, feeding the poor

Choosing a weekly vrat

You do not need to keep all seven. Most people who keep a weekly vrat choose one day, usually the day of the deity they feel closest to or the one their family has always kept, and keep it for a long time. Some take a vow for a fixed number of weeks, such as sixteen Mondays for Shiva, and then complete it with a final puja and a meal for others. If you are unsure, begin with a gentle form of the vrat: a simple meal, a prayer in the evening and a small act of charity, and add more only if it suits your health.

The common practice

The table shows what is commonly done, but families differ. On a fasting day many people eat one meal, or only fruit and milk, until the evening prayer, and break the fast after worship. Others do not fast at all and keep the day by visiting a temple, lighting a lamp or avoiding certain foods. Avoiding salt or certain vegetables on a day is also a way of keeping a vrat. When you break the fast, do it gently, with water, fruit or a light meal.

Health first

The tradition itself says that illness, pregnancy, age and hard work are good reasons to reduce a fast. If you have diabetes, blood pressure or any other condition, or if you take medicine, talk to your doctor before fasting, and read our guide to fasting and health.
