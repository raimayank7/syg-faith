Christianity

A short, neutral introduction: origin, texts, beliefs, practice, holy days and how to visit.

Also called
Christianity
Origin
Roman Palestine, in the first century CE, from the life and teaching of Jesus of Nazareth.
Texts
The Bible: the Old Testament (shared in large part with Judaism) and the New Testament, with the Gospels, the letters and Revelation.
Place of worship
A church, led by a priest, pastor, minister or bishop depending on the denomination. The three main branches are Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant.

Main beliefs

  • One God in three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit (the Trinity).
  • Jesus is the Son of God and Saviour, who died on the cross and rose again on the third day.
  • Salvation through faith in Christ and the grace of God; the commandments to love God and to love one's neighbour.

Practice

Worship on Sunday in church with prayer, hymns, readings and a sermon; Holy Communion (the Eucharist); baptism; Bible reading and prayer at home.

Holy days and festivals

Christmas, Good Friday, Easter, Pentecost, and (in some churches) Advent and Lent.

Visiting a place of worship

Dress neatly, be quiet, follow the lead of the congregation on standing and sitting; communion is usually for members.

A note

Christianity has many denominations with different practices. Many Indian Christians belong to old churches (such as the Syrian Christians of Kerala) as well as to later missions.

Where did Christianity begin?

Roman Palestine, in the first century CE, from the life and teaching of Jesus of Nazareth.

What is the holy book of Christianity?

The Bible: the Old Testament (shared in large part with Judaism) and the New Testament, with the Gospels, the letters and Revelation.

What should a visitor to a place of worship know?

Dress neatly, be quiet, follow the lead of the congregation on standing and sitting; communion is usually for members.

This is a short, neutral introduction written from widely agreed facts. Every faith has many traditions and its members may describe it differently; for a true account, ask a member of that faith. We state no figures and no dates of moveable festivals.
