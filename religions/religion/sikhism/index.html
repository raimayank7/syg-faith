Sikhism

A short, neutral introduction: origin, texts, beliefs, practice, holy days and how to visit.

Also called
Sikhi
Origin
Punjab, at the end of the fifteenth century, with Guru Nanak and nine Gurus after him.
Texts
The Guru Granth Sahib, the scripture, which Sikhs treat as their living Guru; the Dasam Granth is also respected by many.
Place of worship
A gurdwara, which has the Guru Granth Sahib and a langar hall; open to everyone.

Main beliefs

  • One God (Ik Onkar), without form or fear or hatred, to be remembered in the name (naam simran).
  • Equality of all people regardless of caste, creed or gender; honest work (kirat karni), sharing (vand chakna) and service (seva).
  • Life is a path to union with God, not through rituals or asceticism but through a householder's life lived in truth.

Practice

Daily prayers (Nitnem), kirtan (hymn singing), langar (a free community meal) and seva; the five Ks worn by the Khalsa (kesh, kangha, kara, kachera, kirpan).

Holy days and festivals

Vaisakhi, the birthdays (Gurpurabs) of the Gurus, Bandi Chhor Divas, Hola Mohalla.

Visiting a place of worship

Cover your head, take off your shoes, wash your hands, do not bring tobacco, alcohol or meat, sit on the floor, and share langar.

A note

Sikhs are not Hindus or Muslims; Sikhism is an independent religion. Our Gurbani section covers the daily banis.

Where did Sikhism begin?

Punjab, at the end of the fifteenth century, with Guru Nanak and nine Gurus after him.

What is the holy book of Sikhism?

The Guru Granth Sahib, the scripture, which Sikhs treat as their living Guru; the Dasam Granth is also respected by many.

What should a visitor to a place of worship know?

Cover your head, take off your shoes, wash your hands, do not bring tobacco, alcohol or meat, sit on the floor, and share langar.
