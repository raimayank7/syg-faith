Taoism

A short, neutral introduction: origin, texts, beliefs, practice, holy days and how to visit.

Also called
Daoism
Origin
China, with roots in the sixth to fourth centuries BCE.
Texts
The Tao Te Ching, attributed to Laozi, and the Zhuangzi; later religious writings of the Taoist canon.
Place of worship
A Taoist temple (guan) with priests and sometimes monks and nuns.

Main beliefs

  • The Tao ("the way") is the underlying order and source of all things.
  • Wu wei: acting in harmony with nature without forcing.
  • Yin and yang, the complementary pairs, and the search for balance, simplicity and long life.

Practice

Meditation, qigong and tai chi, ritual by Taoist priests, offerings and temple festivals; in China many people mix Taoism with Buddhism and folk religion.

Holy days and festivals

The Chinese New Year and festivals of local deities; the birthday of Laozi.

Visiting a place of worship

Be quiet, make no loud remarks and ask before taking photographs.

A note

Taoism has a philosophical and a religious side that are often separate. Our Tao Te Ching section has the book.
