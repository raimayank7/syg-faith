Jainism

A short, neutral introduction: origin, texts, beliefs, practice, holy days and how to visit.

Also called
Jain Dharma
Origin
India, with a line of 24 teachers called Tirthankaras; Mahavira (about the sixth century BCE) was the last.
Texts
The Agamas, the teachings of Mahavira collected by his disciples; the two main sects, Shvetambara and Digambara, differ on some texts.
Place of worship
A derasar (temple), upashraya (hall for monks); the Dilwara and Ranakpur temples are famous.

Main beliefs

  • Ahimsa, non-violence, towards all living things, in thought, word and deed, is the highest principle.
  • Truth, non-stealing, celibacy (or chastity) and non-possession, and anekantavada, the idea that truth has many sides.
  • Each soul can free itself from karma by right faith, right knowledge and right conduct.

Practice

A strictly vegetarian diet (many avoid root vegetables), fasting, daily meditation and study, and respect for life in every action; monks and nuns take strict vows.

Holy days and festivals

Paryushan and Das Lakshana, Mahavir Jayanti, Diwali (the liberation of Mahavira) and Kartik Purnima.

Visiting a place of worship

Do not bring leather items or food into the temple; take off your shoes; some temples ask women not to enter during menstruation, a custom that varies.

A note

Jainism is a separate religion and is not a branch of Hinduism or Buddhism, though it shares ideas with both.
