Superstition or tradition?

How to think about a belief that comes from a religious or family tradition: the difference between a superstition, a custom and a faith practice, and how to treat other people's beliefs with respect.

One person's superstition is another person's tradition or faith. A belief that is part of a religion, such as not eating certain foods on certain days, is not usually called a superstition by those who hold it. Here are three useful questions.

  1. Does it claim to change events by magic? A custom that marks an occasion is not the same as the claim that a sign predicts or causes luck.
  2. Is anyone harmed? A belief that puts someone at risk, for example by denying treatment to a sick person, is a problem whatever its name.
  3. Is it respected by those who practise it? Treat other people's customs with courtesy, without having to share them.
