Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas

A prayer from the Quran: 112:1-4, 113:1-5, 114:1-6. The Arabic, the transliteration, the English meaning and a note on the context.

Al-Ikhlas 112:1

بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ

Qul huwa Allahu ahadun

Say: He is Allah, the One!

Al-Ikhlas 112:2

ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ

Allahu alssamadu

Allah, the eternally Besought of all!

Al-Ikhlas 112:3

لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ

Lam yalid walam yooladu

He begetteth not nor was begotten.

Al-Ikhlas 112:4

وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ

Walam yakun lahu kufuwan ahadun

And there is none comparable unto Him.

Al-Falaq 113:1

بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ

Qul aAAoothu birabbi alfalaqi

Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of the Daybreak

Al-Falaq 113:2

مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ

Min sharri ma khalaqa

From the evil of that which He created;

Al-Falaq 113:3

وَمِن شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ

Wamin sharri ghasiqin itha waqaba

From the evil of the darkness when it is intense,

Al-Falaq 113:4

وَمِن شَرِّ ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ فِى ٱلْعُقَدِ

Wamin sharri alnnaffathati fee alAAuqadi

And from the evil of malignant witchcraft,

Al-Falaq 113:5

وَمِن شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ

Wamin sharri hasidin itha hasada

And from the evil of the envier when he envieth.

An-Nas 114:1

بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ

Qul aAAoothu birabbi alnnasi

Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind,

An-Nas 114:2

مَلِكِ ٱلنَّاسِ

Maliki alnnasi

The King of mankind,

An-Nas 114:3

إِلَٰهِ ٱلنَّاسِ

Ilahi alnnasi

The god of mankind,

An-Nas 114:4

مِن شَرِّ ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ ٱلْخَنَّاسِ

Min sharri alwaswasi alkhannasi

From the evil of the sneaking whisperer,

An-Nas 114:5

ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ فِى صُدُورِ ٱلنَّاسِ

Allathee yuwaswisu fee sudoori alnnasi

Who whispereth in the hearts of mankind,

An-Nas 114:6

مِنَ ٱلْجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ

Mina aljinnati wa alnnasm

Of the jinn and of mankind.

Context

The last three chapters of the Quran are short. Al-Ikhlas states the oneness of Allah; Al-Falaq and An-Nas ask for refuge from harm.

Explanation

Muslims often recite the three together in the morning, in the evening and before sleep. The first (Al-Ikhlas) is about who Allah is, "He is Allah, the One", and the other two ("Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of the Daybreak" and "Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind") teach a way to ask for protection from all kinds of harm and from whispers of evil. Because the verses are short, they are among the first that children learn.

How to use this page

Read the Arabic if you can, and use the letters beneath it to follow the sounds. The sentence under the transliteration is the English of Pickthall, in the English of 1930; the explanation above is ours. Questions people ask

Where is this prayer in the Quran?

It is in Al-Ikhlas 112:1-4, 113:1-5, 114:1-6.

Do I have to say it in Arabic?

Many Muslims recite the Arabic of the Quran as it is revealed and also pray in their own language. A dua can be made in any language, in your own words, at any time.
