Rabbi a'udhu bika min hamazat ash-shayatin

A prayer from the Quran: 23:97-98. The Arabic, the transliteration, the English meaning and a note on the context.

Al-Muminun 23:97

وَقُل رَّبِّ أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ هَمَزَٰتِ ٱلشَّيَٰطِينِ

Waqul rabbi aAAoothu bika min hamazati alshshayateeni

And say: My Lord! I seek refuge in Thee from suggestions of the evil ones,

Al-Muminun 23:98

وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ رَبِّ أَن يَحْضُرُونِ

WaaAAoothu bika rabbi an yahdurooni

And I seek refuge in Thee, my Lord, lest they be present with me,

Context

The verses are an instruction to the Prophet in the twenty-third surah and give the words of a prayer for refuge.

Explanation

The prayer asks for refuge in Allah from the whispers of evil ones and from their presence. "Hamazat" is the word for their goading or prodding. It is often recited by those troubled by bad thoughts or anger.

How to use this page

Read the Arabic if you can, and use the letters beneath it to follow the sounds. The sentence under the transliteration is the English of Pickthall, in the English of 1930; the explanation above is ours. A dua does not need a special time or place, and you can make it in any language as well. What is a dua?

Questions people ask

Where is this prayer in the Quran?

It is in Al-Muminun 23:97-98.

Do I have to say it in Arabic?

Many Muslims recite the Arabic of the Quran as it is revealed and also pray in their own language. A dua can be made in any language, in your own words, at any time.
