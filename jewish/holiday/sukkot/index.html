Sukkot

The Feast of Booths, 15 to 21 Tishrei

Sukkot, the Feast of Booths, begins on the fifteenth of Tishrei, five days after Yom Kippur, and lasts seven days (with Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah after it). Families build a sukkah, a temporary hut with a roof of branches through which the stars can be seen, to remember the shelters of the Israelites in the wilderness, and eat in it. Four species, the etrog (citron), lulav (palm), hadassim (myrtle) and aravot (willow), are waved in prayer. It is also a harvest festival.

Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah, "rejoicing in the Torah", follow at the end, when the yearly reading of the Torah is completed and begun again.
