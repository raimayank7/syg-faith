Adi Parva: The book of beginnings

Adi Parva of the Mahabharata (the book of beginnings): a summary in plain words, book 1 of 18.

The epic is recited by Vaishampayana to King Janamejaya, as heard from the sage Vyasa, and first told by Ugrashravas. It tells the family history of the Kurus: Shantanu and Ganga, and their son Devavrata, who takes a terrible vow of lifelong celibacy so that his father can marry Satyavati, and is named Bhishma. Satyavati's sons die without heirs; Vyasa fathers Dhritarashtra, Pandu and Vidura. Dhritarashtra is born blind and marries Gandhari, who blindfolds herself; Pandu has five sons by Kunti and Madri through the gods: Yudhishthira, Bhima, Arjuna, Nakula and Sahadeva, while Gandhari has a hundred sons, the eldest Duryodhana. The boys learn arms from Drona; Arjuna becomes the finest archer. Karna, Kunti's son from the sun god before her marriage, is raised by a charioteer. The Pandavas survive a plot to burn them in a house of lac, Arjuna wins Draupadi at her swayamvara, and the five brothers marry her. The kingdom is divided, and the Pandavas build Indraprastha.

What to notice

The first book is the longest in its range of stories: it opens with the telling of the epic itself and then goes back through the ancestors of the Kurus, including Bharata, Yayati and Shantanu. It is also where the roots of the war appear: the birth of the hundred Kauravas, the rivalry of the cousins, Drona's school, Karna's challenge and the house of lac. The marriage to Draupadi at the end of the book sets the Pandavas up as a strong family with powerful allies.

This is book 1 of the eighteen parvas of the Mahabharata. See all eighteen and the characters. This summary is our own retelling; tellings of the epic differ.
