The Mahabharata

The story of the Pandavas and the Kauravas, retold book by book, with the characters.

Start with the story in eighteen books, then read the books one by one.

How to read this section

The Mahabharata is the longest epic poem in the world, with about 100,000 verses, and it cannot be read in an afternoon. This section gives the story in our own words, in the eighteen books (parvas) in which it is traditionally divided. If you want the whole shape of the story first, read the Mahabharata in eighteen books; then read the books in order. The first books explain how the two branches of the Kuru family came to be rivals, the middle books tell the war at Kurukshetra, and the last books tell its aftermath and the end of the Pandavas.

If you would rather begin with people, the characters pages give a portrait of sixteen of the main figures, including Draupadi, Karna and Krishna, with what they do in the story and how they are seen. The Gita page explains where the Bhagavad Gita fits. The retelling is our own and quotes no verse; tellings differ on many episodes, and this site has not been read by a scholar.

The eighteen books

  • Adi Parva: The book of beginnings
  • Sabha Parva: The book of the assembly hall
  • Vana Parva: The book of the forest
  • Virata Parva: The book of Virata
  • Udyoga Parva: The book of effort
  • Bhishma Parva: The book of Bhishma
  • Drona Parva: The book of Drona
  • Karna Parva: The book of Karna
  • Shalya Parva: The book of Shalya
  • Sauptika Parva: The book of the night attack
  • Stri Parva: The book of the women
  • Shanti Parva: The book of peace
  • Anushasana Parva: The book of instruction
  • Ashvamedhika Parva: The book of the horse sacrifice
  • Ashramavasika Parva: The book of the hermitage
  • Mausala Parva: The book of the clubs
  • Mahaprasthanika Parva: The book of the great journey
  • Svargarohana Parva: The book of the ascent to heaven
