Ramadan at work and school

How colleagues, managers and teachers can support Muslim co-workers and students who are fasting: timing, meetings, meals and kindness, without singling anyone out.

If a colleague or a student is fasting, small adjustments make a big difference. The aim is respect, not special treatment.

  • Timing: try not to put important meetings or exams at the end of the day, when energy is lowest, if you can choose. Early starts may suit people who rise before dawn.
  • Meals and events: do not schedule lunches. If a team lunch cannot be avoided, a fasting colleague can still come and sit. Offering to move a party to an evening iftar can be kind; let colleagues decide.
  • Breaks: allow flexible break times at sunset for iftar and short breaks for prayer.
  • Eating in front of someone who fasts: it is not wrong, and most people who fast do not mind; it is simply considerate to be quiet about it.
  • Do not ask whether they are tired or how they cope. Instead ask whether anything would help.
  • Eid: the day is fixed by the moon sighting, and it may be known only the night before. A little notice flexibility for a day off helps everybody.

Dates of Ramadan depend on the sighting of the moon and can differ by a day between countries and communities (in India, often a day after Saudi Arabia). Times are calculated and may differ by a minute or two from your mosque's printed timetable, which often adds a few minutes of caution to the start of the fast. This page is not a religious ruling; follow your local mosque or a trusted scholar.
