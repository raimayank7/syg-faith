Ramadan in Los Angeles over three years

The Islamic calendar is lunar, so Ramadan begins about eleven days earlier in the solar calendar each year and slowly moves through every season. For Los Angeles, which lies at latitude 34.1 degrees north, that matters for the length of the fast, because the gap between Fajr and Maghrib changes with the season. The table compares the three years of this timetable; the average is taken over every day of the month.

Ramadan 1448 begins in February, which is winter in Los Angeles; 1449 begins in January (winter) and 1450 in January (winter). The longest average fast of the three is in Ramadan 1448 (12 h 23 min), the shortest in Ramadan 1450 (11 h 43 min), a difference of about 40 minutes between the two on an average day. Within one Ramadan the fast changes by about 2.0 minutes a day, so the last day of the month differs from the first by about 55 minutes.

Planning the day

On the first day of Ramadan 1448 sehri must end by 05:34 and iftar is at 17:30 local time. That leaves about 12 h 04 min between iftar and the next sehri to eat, drink and sleep. Because the clock times shift by a minute or so every day, a printed table is more reliable than remembering the first day, and the table above gives every day. If you travel to another city during Ramadan, use the table of the city you are in: even a few hundred kilometres east or west moves the times by several minutes. The Islamic Society of North America method used here is the usual choice for United States; your mosque may publish slightly different times for caution.
