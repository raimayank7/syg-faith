Where do omens come from?

A short account of how omen-watching grew in India: the old texts on signs, the role of farmers and travellers, and why the beliefs survive.

Omen-watching is as old as the written record. The Vedic and later Sanskrit literature has sections on signs (nimitta and shakuna), and old texts on astrology and on royal rule include long lists of them. In everyday life, however, most beliefs are older than any book and come from observation: farmers, shepherds and travellers noticed that birds and animals react to weather, danger and strangers, and that is useful to know.

Why they last

Omens survive because they do several jobs at once. They give a pause before a risky start. They offer a small ritual (curd and sugar, a sip of water) that calms nerves. And they are a shared language in the family: when an aunt says "a guest is coming", everybody smiles. None of that depends on whether the omen is true.

Regional differences

The same sign can mean different things in different places. A snake crossing is a bad sign in one region and a good one in another; the right eye twitching is good for men in one place and for women in another. This is itself a sign that the readings come from local habit.

A sensible view

Enjoy the stories, keep the small rituals that you like, and make real decisions about travel, health and money on real information. Never cancel a medical appointment, an exam or a journey because of an omen.

This page reports popular beliefs for interest. Omens do not predict anything, and nothing here is advice. Please do not cancel anything important, such as a medical appointment, an exam or a journey, because of an omen.
